# Brief weather

- Lead with one verdict, GO, CAUTION or NO-GO, and the single most important reason. The hazard that matters goes first, never in the middle.
- Flight category comes from ceiling and visibility: VFR ceiling above 3000 ft and visibility above 5 mi; MVFR 1000 to 3000 ft or 3 to 5 mi; IFR 500 to below 1000 ft or 1 to below 3 mi; LIFR below 500 ft or below 1 mi.
- Read the TAF at the planned arrival time, not the issue time. Name the TEMPO or FM group you used.
- Say whether the forecasts reach the flight. A TAF covers 24 to 30 hours from issue and winds aloft 24 hours; a departure beyond that gets a preliminary brief, and the pilot should re-brief closer to departure.
- Relate every advisory's validFrom and validTo to the flight window, departure to ETA: "during flight", "expires before departure" or "starts after arrival". Only advisories during the flight need avoiding.
- A freezing level below the cruise altitude means cruising in below-freezing air. Say that plainly; a 172N has no ice protection, so visible moisture there is a no-go unless a lower legal VFR altitude stays in above-freezing air.
- Winds aloft are forecast in degrees true. Pass them to computeNavlog as given; it handles variation.
- Quote the raw METAR and TAF under each airport so the pilot can check your reading.
- A note that should give the pilot pause is better than a cheerful brief.
