# Privacy

B4.run is built by Cacheplane. This page explains what the B4.run website and the `b4` CLI record, what happens when you give us your email, who processes that data on our behalf, and how to opt out or ask us to delete it.

## What the website records

When you browse b4.run we record anonymous signals about how the site is used: pageviews, clicks on calls to action, use of copy buttons, when search is opened, and how long a page stays visible. These signals are tied to a random identifier stored in your browser, not to your name or email. There are no accounts on this site.

We use these signals to see which pages help and which don't, so we can improve the docs.

## What the `b4` CLI will report

Once CLI reporting ships, the `b4` command will send a small anonymous event when you run it: the command name, the CLI version, your operating system, and whether the command succeeded or failed. It will not send your code, file names, prompts, or environment values.

To opt out, set either of these environment variables:

```sh
B4_TELEMETRY_DISABLED=1
DO_NOT_TRACK=1
```

Nothing is reported by `create-b4-app` or by an app you build and run with B4.run.

## If you give us your email

If you subscribe, we will use your address to send occasional product updates and, sometimes, a personal note from Brian. Every message includes a one-click unsubscribe link. Your email address is stored with our email provider and is never sent to PostHog.

## Who processes this data

We use these subprocessors:

- Vercel hosts the website.
- Neon hosts the database that holds our contact list.
- PostHog (United States region) receives the anonymous website and CLI signals.
- Resend delivers email.
- Google Calendar handles scheduling when you book time with us.

## How long we keep it

Contacts are kept until you unsubscribe or ask us to delete them. Anonymous signals are kept for the retention period configured in our PostHog project.
